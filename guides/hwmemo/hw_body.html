SECTION A · Rotation about a fixed axis (L11)

Spot it: the body is pinned at a fixed point O. Flow: write $\vec r$ from O to the point → $\vec v = \vec\omega\times\vec r$ (⊥ to OP, in the sense of ω) → $\vec a = \vec\alpha\times\vec r - \omega^2\vec r$ ($a_t = r\alpha$ ⊥ OP in the sense of α; $a_n = r\omega^2$ towards O) → magnitude and direction.
Shortcut: $\omega\vec k\times(x\vec i + y\vec j) = \omega(-y\vec i + x\vec j)$, with CCW positive.
{{crop:p52}}

Solution

[Senses: the slide's answer corresponds to ω counter-clockwise and α clockwise, so $\vec\omega = \omega\vec k$ and $\vec\alpha = -\alpha\vec k$.]

Position of A from O: $\vec r_A = b\vec i - h\vec j$
$\vec v_A = \omega\vec k\times(b\vec i - h\vec j) = \omega(h\vec i + b\vec j)$
$\vec\alpha\times\vec r_A = -\alpha\vec k\times(b\vec i - h\vec j) = -\alpha(h\vec i + b\vec j)$
$-\omega^2\vec r_A = -\omega^2 b\,\vec i + \omega^2 h\,\vec j$
$\vec a_A = -(b\omega^2 + h\alpha)\vec i + (h\omega^2 - b\alpha)\vec j$

[Check the picture: $\vec v_A$ is ⊥ to OA ($\vec v_A\cdot\vec r_A = \omega(hb - bh) = 0$ ✓), $a_n$ points from A back to O, and $a_t$ is opposite to $\vec v_A$ because α opposes ω (it's slowing down).]

$\vec v_A = \omega(h\vec i + b\vec j)$ · $\vec a_A = -(b\omega^2 + h\alpha)\vec i + (h\omega^2 - b\alpha)\vec j$

{{crop:p517}}

Solution

[Senses from the figure: the ω arrow sweeps left → right under O, so ω = 2 rad/s CCW. The α arrow sweeps the other way, so α = 5 rad/s² CW.]

Geometry

OB = 0.5 m at 15° to the vertical; BA = 0.3 m; the angle at the bend is 120°.
Cosine rule: $OA^2 = 0.5^2 + 0.3^2 - 2(0.5)(0.3)\cos 120° = 0.49 \Rightarrow OA = 0.7$ m
Components: $\vec r_A = (0.5\sin 15° + 0.3\cos 15°)\vec i - (0.5\cos 15° + 0.3\sin 15°)\vec j = 0.4192\vec i - 0.5606\vec j$ m

Velocity

$\vec v_A = 2\vec k\times(0.4192\vec i - 0.5606\vec j) = 1.121\vec i + 0.838\vec j$ m/s
$|\vec v_A| = \omega\,OA = 2(0.7) = 1.40$ m/s ✓

Acceleration

$\vec\alpha\times\vec r_A = -5\vec k\times\vec r_A = -2.803\vec i - 2.096\vec j$
$-\omega^2\vec r_A = -4\vec r_A = -1.677\vec i + 2.242\vec j$
$\vec a_A = -4.48\vec i + 0.147\vec j$ m/s²
Check: $a_t = \alpha\,OA = 3.5$, $a_n = \omega^2 OA = 2.8$, so $|\vec a_A| = \sqrt{3.5^2 + 2.8^2} = 4.48$ m/s² ✓

$\vec v_A = 1.121\vec i + 0.838\vec j$ m/s (1.40 m/s) · $\vec a_A = -4.48\vec i + 0.147\vec j$ m/s² (4.48 m/s²)

Solution

SECTION B · Absolute motion: geometry, then differentiate (L12)

Spot it: "as a function of x", "in terms of θ", or a contact or slot geometry. Flow: (1) draw the geometry at a general position and name the changing variables; (2) write one relation between the angle and the distance (tan, sin, sine or cosine rule); (3) differentiate with respect to time using the chain rule; (4) substitute the known rate, with its sign (is x growing or shrinking?); (5) decide CW/CCW from how the angle changes.
{{crop:p542}}

Solution

The bar rests on the fixed corner P, at height h above A. With θ the bar's angle to the horizontal at A:
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{x}\;\Rightarrow\;\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{h}{x}$
Differentiate: $\dot\theta = \dfrac{1}{1 + h^2/x^2}\left(-\dfrac{h}{x^2}\right)\dot x = -\dfrac{h\,\dot x}{x^2 + h^2}$
The drum winds the cable in, so A moves left towards the corner: $\dot x = -r\omega_0$
$\omega = \dot\theta = \dfrac{r h\,\omega_0}{x^2 + h^2}$

[Sense: as x shrinks, θ grows and the bar gets steeper. A swings under P towards the left, so the bar turns clockwise. The bar's angular velocity is the rate of change of any line fixed to it, here θ.]

ω = rhω₀/(x² + h²), clockwise

{{crop:p544}}

Solution

The lever is tangent to the disk, so the radius to the contact point is ⊥ to OA and has length r. Its centre is a horizontal distance x from O, so in the right triangle:
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{r}{x}\;\Rightarrow\;\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{r}{x}$
Differentiate: $\dot\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - r^2/x^2}}\left(-\dfrac{r}{x^2}\right)\dot x$
The disk moves right, towards O, so x shrinks: $\dot x = -v$
$\omega = \dfrac{r v}{x^2\sqrt{1 - r^2/x^2}} = \dfrac{r v}{x\sqrt{x^2 - r^2}} = \dfrac{v}{x\sqrt{(x/r)^2 - 1}}$

[Sense: θ grows, so A lifts. A point to the left of O moving up means clockwise.]

ω = v / [x√((x/r)² − 1)], clockwise

{{crop:p556}}

Solution

[Wheel C turns with the slot O₂P, so ω₂ is the rate of change of the angle β of line O₂P. Write β in terms of θ (the angle of O₁P), then differentiate.]

With $r = O_1P = 200/\sqrt2 = 141.4$ mm and $d = O_1O_2 = 200$ mm, P is at $(r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta)$ from O₁:
$\tan\beta = \dfrac{r\sin\theta}{d - r\cos\theta}$
Differentiate (quotient rule, then simplify):
$\omega_2 = \dot\beta = \omega_1\,\dfrac{r(d\cos\theta - r)}{d^2 + r^2 - 2dr\cos\theta}$
At θ = 20°: numerator $= 0.1414(0.18794 - 0.14142) = 0.006579$; denominator $= 0.06 - 0.053157 = 0.006843$
$\omega_2 = 2(0.9614) = 1.923$ rad/s

[Sanity checks: at engagement (θ = 45°) $d\cos\theta = r$, so ω₂ = 0 and the wheel starts smoothly. On the line of centres (θ = 0) the ratio peaks at r/(d − r) = 2.41. Senses: wheel A turns CW, so P sweeps down towards the line O₁O₂. That rotates slot O₂P, and wheel C, counter-clockwise.]

SECTION C · Instantaneous centre of zero velocity (L13)

Spot it: "using instantaneous centres", or a velocity-only question on a link. Flow: (1) for each link, find two points whose velocity directions you know; (2) draw the line ⊥ to each velocity: they cross at the IC (C); (3) get distances by trig; (4) ω = v/rknown, then v = ω r for the point you want, ⊥ to its line from C; (5) carry the shared pin's velocity to the next link.
The trap in 5/108 and 5/117: a roller pressed against a moving surface only shares that surface's velocity ⊥ to the surface. It can slide along it.
{{crop:p5119}}

Solution

Link OBD (fixed pivot O)

$\vec v_D$ is ⊥ OD, i.e. 60° from the horizontal. Its downward component is 0.6 m/s, so $v_D = 0.6/\sin 60° = 0.6928$ m/s.
$\omega_{OD} = v_D/OD = 0.6928/1.2 = 0.5774$ rad/s CW (θ increasing); $\;v_B = 0.8\,\omega_{OD} = 0.4619$ m/s, ⊥ OB

Link AB: locate its IC

B = (0.6928, −0.4) m from O. A is on the shaft with AB = 0.7: $x_A = 0.6928 + \sqrt{0.7^2 - 0.4^2} = 1.2673$ m
⊥ to $\vec v_A$ (horizontal) → the vertical line through A. ⊥ to $\vec v_B$ → the line OB itself, extended.
They meet at C, at depth $1.2673\tan 30° = 0.7317$ m below A. So $r_{A/C} = 0.7317$ m and $r_{B/C} = OC - OB = 1.4634 - 0.8 = 0.6634$ m.
$\omega_{AB} = v_B/r_{B/C} = 0.4619/0.6634 = 0.696$ rad/s
$v_A = \omega_{AB}\,r_{A/C} = 0.696(0.7317) = 0.509$ m/s, to the left

[Direction: B moves down-left, so AB turns CCW about C. C is directly below A, so A moves left. Vector check: $\vec v_B + \omega_{AB}\vec k\times\vec r_{A/B}$ with ωAB = 0.696 CCW gives $\vec v_A = -0.509\vec i$ ✓]

vA = 0.509 m/s ←

Solution

[Read the constraints carefully. B is pinned to the rod of the fixed cylinder F, so $\vec v_B$ is horizontal. D runs in a vertical guide, so $\vec v_D$ is vertical. A is a roller pressed against the plunger's vertical plate, so only its horizontal velocity is 2 m/s. It can roll up or down the plate.]

IC of ABD: ⊥ to $\vec v_B$ → the vertical line through B; ⊥ to $\vec v_D$ → the horizontal line through D. So C = (xB, yD).
A horizontal velocity at A comes only from A's vertical distance from C: $v_{A,x} = \omega\,(y_A - y_C) = \omega(0.3\sin 30°) = 0.15\,\omega$
$\omega = 2/0.15 = 13.33$ rad/s CW
$v_D = \omega\,(x_D - x_C) = 13.33(0.2\cos 30°) = 13.33(0.1732) = 2.31$ m/s ↓

[Full velocity of A: $\vec v_A = 2\vec i + 1.155\vec j$ m/s. The roller climbs the plate at 1.155 m/s while the plate pushes it at 2 m/s. Using A as a pin, i.e. assuming vA is horizontal, puts the IC on the vertical through A and gives the wrong vD = 3.46 m/s.]

Solution

$\vec v_F$ is ⊥ DF (DF at 45°), so its vertical part is $v_F\sin 45° = 2$, giving $v_F = 2.828$ m/s

{{crop:p5120}}

Solution

[Read the figure: B is the central (sun) gear turning about O; the arm OA carries planet gear A (radius a/2) at distance a. The planet meshes with B (radius a/2) and with ring D (radius 3a/2). Work along the planet's horizontal diameter, from the sun contact (x = a/2) through A (x = a) to the ring contact (x = 3a/2).]

Arm at 90 rev/min CW: the planet centre A (on the right) moves down at $90a$ (in a·rev/min units).
The planet is a rigid disc, so its velocity varies linearly along that diameter: the same velocity-line idea as the pulleys.

(a) Ring D fixed

Ring contact: v = 0 (that's the planet's IC). A: 90a ↓, so the sun contact (twice as far from the IC) moves at 180a ↓
$\omega_B = 180a/(a/2) = 360$ rev/min CW

(b) Ring D at 80 rev/min CCW

Ring contact: $80(3a/2) = 120a$ ↑. A: 90a ↓. The change across half a diameter is 210a, so the sun contact moves at $90a + 210a = 300a$ ↓
$\omega_B = 300a/(a/2) = 600$ rev/min CW. The planet's IC now sits inside the planet, 0.214a beyond A.
