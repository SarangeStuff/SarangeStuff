SECTION A [50 marks]

Question 1 [9] · n–t coordinates

A model train (a particle) travels along a curved path. At A the speed is 3 m/s, decreasing at 0.75 m/s². At B the speed is increasing at 0.7 m/s². (a) Sketch the directions of the acceleration components at A and B. (b) If the magnitude of the acceleration stays constant, find the velocity at B.

Solution

(a)

{{fig:s1}}
At A: $a_t$ points backwards (opposite to $v_A$), because the train is slowing {{t}}; $a_n$ points up, towards the centre of the 40 m dip {{t}}
At B: $a_t$ points forwards (speeding up); $a_n$ points down, towards the centre of the 20 m hump {{t}}

[Why: $a_n$ always points to the inside of the bend, where the centre of curvature is. $a_t$ follows the change in speed, not the direction of travel.]

(b)

At A: $a_n = v_A^2/\rho_A = 3^2/40 = 0.225$ m/s² {{t}}
$|\vec a| = \sqrt{0.75^2 + 0.225^2} = 0.783$ m/s², and this is the same at B {{t}}
At B: $a_n = \sqrt{0.783^2 - 0.7^2} = 0.351$ m/s² {{t2}}
$v_B^2 = \rho_B\,a_n = 20(0.351)$ {{t}}, so $v_B = 2.65$ m/s {{t}}

Question 2 [9] · constrained motion

Block A and weight B are attached to the pulley system shown. If the velocity of B is 3.15 m/s downwards, find the velocity of A. The upper surface of A stays horizontal.

Solution

Datum at the ceiling; $s_A$ and $s_B$ measured downwards to A and to B {{t2}}
Trace the one cable: ceiling → down under A's 1st pulley → up over a ceiling pulley → down under A's 2nd → up → down under A's 3rd → up over the top pulley → across → down to B {{t}}
Each of A's 3 pulleys carries 2 vertical strands, so 6 strands get longer when A drops {{t}}
$L = 6s_A + s_B + \text{constant}$ (the horizontal run and the arcs never change) {{t}}

[Why not 5 or 7: count only the strands that end on a pulley attached to A. All six change length by exactly $\Delta s_A$ when A moves; the strand to B changes by $\Delta s_B$. Nothing is subtracted.]

Differentiate: $6v_A + v_B = 0$ {{t}}
$v_A = -\dfrac{3.15}{6}$ {{t}} $= -0.525$ m/s {{t}}
Negative in a "down is positive" convention, so A rises at 0.525 m/s {{t}}

[Velocity-line check: B moves down at 3.15, so the strands work in parallel and A, held by 6 of them, moves at 1/6 of the rope speed.]

Question 3 [9] · rectilinear motion, a(v)

The acceleration of a particle is $a = -k\sqrt v$. At t = 0, x = 0 and v = 81 m/s; when x = 18 m, v = 36 m/s. Find (a) the velocity when x = 20 m, and (b) the time for the particle to come to rest.

Solution

(a)

[a is given as a function of v and we want v at a position, so use v dv = a dx.]

$v\dfrac{dv}{dx} = -k\sqrt v$ {{t}}
$\displaystyle\int_{81}^{v} v^{1/2}\,dv = -k\int_0^x dx$ {{t}}, so $\tfrac23\left(v^{3/2} - 729\right) = -kx$ ……[1] {{t}}
x = 18, v = 36: $\tfrac23(216 - 729) = -18k$, so $k = 19$ {{t}}
From [1] at x = 20: $v^{3/2} = 729 - \tfrac32(19)(20) = 159$ {{t}}, so $v = 159^{2/3} = 29.3$ m/s {{t}}

(b)

[Now we want time, so use a = dv/dt.]

$\displaystyle\int_{81}^{v} v^{-1/2}\,dv = -k\int_0^t dt$ {{t}}, so $2(\sqrt v - 9) = -kt$ {{t}}
At rest (v = 0): $t = 18/19 = 0.947$ s {{t}}

Question 4 [6] · relative velocity of separate bodies

A water drop falls from rest at A of an overpass. After dropping 6 m it strikes the windshield at B of a car travelling at 100 km/h. The windshield is inclined 50° from the vertical. Find the acute angle, relative to the windshield normal n, at which the drop strikes.

Solution

Free fall: $v_D = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2(9.81)(6)} = 10.85$ m/s ↓ {{t}}
$v_C = 100/3.6 = 27.78$ m/s → {{t}}

[What the windshield "feels" is the drop's velocity relative to the car. The drop and the car are separate bodies, so there's no ⊥ rule: just subtract.]

$\vec v_{D/C} = \vec v_D - \vec v_C = (-27.78,\ -10.85)$ m/s {{t}}
It points backwards and down at $\tan^{-1}(10.85/27.78) = 21.3°$ below the horizontal {{t}}
The windshield is 50° from the vertical, so its normal n is 50° above the horizontal {{t}}
Angle to the normal: $50° - 21.3° = 28.7°$ {{t}}

Question 5 [17] · relative velocity and acceleration

Three bars are pinned as shown. Arm AB has a constant clockwise angular velocity of 6 rad/s. For this instant: (a) determine the angular velocity of BDE; (b) draw a scaled relative acceleration diagram of BDE and use it to find the angular acceleration of BDE; (c) calculate the acceleration of E.

Geometry: A(0, 0), B(0, 90), C(225, 0), D(225, 180), E(450, 270) mm. B, D and E are collinear, and AB and CD are vertical.

Solution

(a) Velocities

$v_B = \omega_{AB}\,AB = 6(0.09) = 0.54$ m/s, ⊥ AB. AB is vertical and turns CW, so B moves → {{t}}
CD is vertical, so $\vec v_D$ (⊥ CD) is also horizontal {{t}}
The perpendiculars to $\vec v_B$ and $\vec v_D$ are two parallel vertical lines: they never meet, so there's no IC {{t}}
BDE is translating at this instant: $\omega_{BDE} = 0$ {{t}}
So $v_D = v_E = 0.54$ m/s →, and $\omega_{CD} = 0.54/0.18 = 3$ rad/s CW {{t}}

[Check with relative velocity: $\vec v_D = \vec v_B + \vec v_{D/B}$. Both absolute velocities are horizontal, but $\vec v_{D/B}$ must be ⊥ BD (not horizontal). The only way the equation balances is $\vec v_{D/B} = 0$.]

(b) Accelerations

$(\vec a_D)_n + (\vec a_D)_t = \vec a_B + (\vec a_{D/B})_n + (\vec a_{D/B})_t$ {{t}}
$a_B = \omega_{AB}^2\,AB = 36(0.09) = 3.24$ m/s², from B towards A (↓). There's no t part, because ωAB is constant {{t}}
$(a_D)_n = \omega_{CD}^2\,CD = 9(0.18) = 1.62$ m/s², from D towards C (↓) {{t}}
$(a_{D/B})_n = \omega_{BDE}^2\,BD = 0$ {{t}}
Unknown sizes: $(\vec a_D)_t$ is horizontal (⊥ CD); $(\vec a_{D/B})_t$ is ⊥ BD, at 111.8° {{t}}
{{fig:s5a}}
Scaled diagram as shown {{t}}
Vertical balance: $1.62 = 3.24 - (a_{D/B})_t\sin 68.2°$, so $(a_{D/B})_t = 1.745$ m/s², and $\alpha_{BDE} = 1.745/0.2423 = 7.2$ rad/s² {{t}}
Sense: $(\vec a_{D/B})_t$ points up-left at D, which is up-right of B, so it turns BDE counter-clockwise {{t}}

(c) Acceleration of E

$\vec a_E = \vec a_B + (\vec a_{E/B})_t$ (the n part is zero, because ωBDE = 0) {{t}}
$(a_{E/B})_t = \alpha\,BE = 7.2(0.4847) = 3.49$ m/s², ⊥ BE. On the diagram, e lies on line bd extended to twice the length, since BE = 2BD {{t}}
Vector form: $(0, -3.24) + 7.2\,\vec k\times(0.45,\ 0.18) = (-1.296,\ 0)$, so $a_E = 1.30$ m/s² {{t}}, horizontal, to the left {{t}}

Question 6 [6] · ΣF = ma with static friction

The coefficient of static friction between the flat bed of the truck and the crate is 0.32. Find the minimum distance s needed to stop the truck from 80 km/h, with constant deceleration, without the crate slipping.

Solution

FBD of the crate: the only horizontal force is friction from the bed, acting backwards {{t}}

[Why friction decelerates the crate: the bed slows down, and the crate tends to slide forwards relative to it. Static friction opposes that relative motion, so it points backwards.]

No slip, at most: $F_{max} = \mu_s N = \mu_s mg$ {{t}}, so $a_{max} = \mu_s g = 0.32(9.81) = 3.14$ m/s² {{t}}
$v_0 = 80/3.6 = 22.22$ m/s {{t}}
$0 = v_0^2 - 2as$, so $s = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2a} = \dfrac{22.22^2}{2(3.14)}$ {{t}} $= 78.7$ m {{t}}

[The 2.5 m dimension and the crate's mass aren't needed: m cancels.]

Question 7 [18] · work–energy in two stages, with a pulley constraint

The system is released from rest. The 13 kg cylinder falls through the hole in the support, but the 15 kg collar is removed when it hits the support. Find the total distance s the 45 kg block moves up the incline before coming instantaneously to rest. μk = 0.33, and the pulley mass is negligible.

Solution

Constraint and forces

The block carries a pulley, and two cable strands pull it up the slope. If the block moves d, the cylinder moves 2d, so $v_{block} = \tfrac12 v_{cyl}$ {{t2}}
$N = 45g\cos 25° = 400.1$ N {{t}}; friction $= 0.33N = 132.0$ N, acting down the slope (the block moves up) {{t}}
Weight component along the slope: $45g\sin 25° = 186.6$ N {{t}}
Check it moves: 28 kg hanging gives 274.7 N per strand, i.e. 549 N on the block, which beats 186.6 + 132.0 = 318.6 N {{t}}

[If you treat it as a single cable, the block would never move (274.7 < 318.6). That's the hint that the 2:1 pulley matters.]

Stage 1: release to the collar hitting the support (cylinder falls 1.15 m, block moves 0.575 m)

$T_1 + V_1 + U'_{1\text{-}2} = T_2 + V_2$ for the whole system (the cable tension does no net work) {{t}}
Cylinder + collar fall: $28(9.81)(1.15) = 315.9$ J released {{t}}
Block rises: $186.6(0.575) = 107.3$ J stored {{t}}; friction work: $-132.0(0.575) = -75.9$ J {{t}}
$T_2 = \tfrac12(28)v_c^2 + \tfrac12(45)(v_c/2)^2 = 19.63\,v_c^2$ {{t}}
$315.9 - 107.3 - 75.9 = 19.63\,v_c^2$, so $v_c = 2.60$ m/s and $v_{block} = 1.30$ m/s {{t}}

Stage 2: collar removed, the 13 kg cylinder continues until the block stops

KE at the start of stage 2, without the collar: $\tfrac12(13)(2.60)^2 + \tfrac12(45)(1.30)^2 = 43.9 + 38.0 = 82.0$ J {{t}}

[The collar's KE leaves with the collar. Per the question, its removal doesn't change the velocities of the cylinder and block.]

Block moves a further d, so the cylinder falls 2d {{t}}: gravity on the cylinder gives $13(9.81)(2d) = 255.1d$ {{t}}
Losses: $(186.6 + 132.0)d = 318.6d$ {{t}}
$82.0 + 255.1d - 318.6d = 0$, so $d = 1.29$ m {{t}}
$s = 0.575 + 1.29 = 1.87$ m {{t}}

Question 8 [4] · mass moment of inertia of a rod assembly

The slender rods are welded together as shown. Each 100 mm segment has a mass of 0.12 kg. Compute the mass moment of inertia about the z-axis.

Solution

Use $I_z = \sum\int(x^2 + y^2)\,dm$: each rod's contribution depends on its distance from the z-axis {{t}}
RodPosition relative to zI (kg·m²)
origin → junctionlies on the z-axis0
junction → −y, and junction → +y⊥ to z, with one end on the axis: $\tfrac13 mL^2$ each2 × 0.0004
vertical rod (along x) at y = −0.1$\tfrac13 mL^2 + m(0.1)^2$0.0016
rod parallel to z at y = +0.1every point is 0.1 m away: $m(0.1)^2$0.0012
$\tfrac13 mL^2 = \tfrac13(0.12)(0.1)^2 = 0.0004$ kg·m² {{t}}
The offset rods: $0.0004 + 0.12(0.01) = 0.0016$ and $0.12(0.01) = 0.0012$ {{t}}
$I_z = 0 + 0.0008 + 0.0016 + 0.0012 = 3.60\times10^{-3}$ kg·m² {{t}}

[Reading the 3-D figure: the first rod from the origin runs along the z-axis. The rod parallel to z contributes m d², not a ⅓mL² term, because its length runs along the axis.]

Question 9 [6] · plastic impact, momentum in components

Two cars collide at right angles on icy roads. Car A (1300 kg) moves at 45 km/h; car B has a mass of 1500 kg. They become entangled and move together at v in the direction shown (25° from the y-axis). Find the velocity of B just before the impact.

Solution

Icy road, so no external horizontal impulse: momentum is conserved in x and y {{t}}
x: $1500\,v_B = 2800\,v\sin 25°$ {{t}}
y: $1300(45) = 2800\,v\cos 25°$ {{t}}
Divide: $\tan 25° = \dfrac{1500\,v_B}{1300(45)}$ {{t}}
$v_B = 18.2$ km/h {{t}}; $\;v = 23.1$ km/h {{t}}

[No units conversion needed: km/h cancels throughout. No e is needed either: "entangled" means e = 0.]

Question 10 [16] · rolling with friction, cable on the shaft

The 30 kg spool (ro = 450 mm, k̄ = 275 mm, shaft ri = 200 mm) is at rest on the 20° incline when a tension T is applied to the cable wrapped around the shaft. μs = 0.45, μk = 0.30. (a) Find T and the friction force that keep G at rest. (b) Find the maximum T for which the spool rolls up the slope without slipping, with an FBD. (c) Find the corresponding aG.

Solution

{{fig:s10}}

[Reading the figure: the cable leaves from the bottom of the shaft, parallel to the slope, pulling up the slope. Its moment about G turns the spool the "rolling down" way, so friction must act down the slope to balance it.]

(a) Equilibrium

FBD: mg, N, F at the contact, T at the bottom of the shaft {{t}}
$\sum M_G = 0$: $T r_i = F r_o$, so $F = 0.444\,T$ {{t}}
$\sum F_\parallel = 0$: $T - F - mg\sin 20° = 0$ {{t}}
$T(1 - 0.444) = 30(9.81)\sin 20° = 100.7$, so $T = 181$ N {{t}}
$F = 80.5$ N down the slope. Check: $\mu_s N = 0.45(276.6) = 124.5$ N > 80.5 N, so no slip ✓ {{t}}

[Why not $F = \mu_s N$ here: friction only reaches $\mu_s N$ on the point of slipping. At rest it's whatever the balance equations need.]

(b) On the point of slipping, rolling up

FBD and KD as shown {{t}}
Rolling without slip: $a_G = r_o\alpha$ {{t}}
On the point of slipping: $F = \mu_s N = 124.5$ N {{t}}, still acting down the slope {{t}}
$\sum F_\parallel$: $T - 124.5 - 100.7 = 30\,a_G$ {{t}}
$\sum M_G$ (in the rolling-up sense): $124.5(0.45) - 0.2\,T = 30(0.275)^2\dfrac{a_G}{0.45}$ {{t}}
Solve: $T = 255$ N {{t}}

(c)

$a_G = \dfrac{255 - 124.5 - 100.7}{30}$ {{t}} $= 0.995$ m/s² {{t}}, up the slope {{t}}
$\alpha = a_G/r_o = 2.21$ rad/s² {{t}}

[Check the direction: friction's moment (124.5 × 0.45 = 56.0 N·m) beats the cable's (255 × 0.2 = 51.0 N·m), so the net moment spins the spool the rolling-up way ✓]
