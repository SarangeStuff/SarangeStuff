The question [17 marks]

End A of the uniform bar AB (length 1.2 m) moves along the horizontal track with a constant velocity $v_A = 3$ m/s to the right. End B is guided by a circular track of radius 0.5 m, which blends smoothly into the horizontal track. At the instant shown, the radius to B makes 60° with the vertical through the centre of the guide.

Using scaled vector diagrams, determine:

  1. the velocity of B and the angular velocity of AB;
  2. the tangential acceleration of B and the angular acceleration of AB.

Explained memo

Step 0 · Read the constraints before drawing anything

A rides on a straight track at constant speed. So $\vec v_A = 3$ m/s →, and because the speed never changes, $\vec a_A = 0$.
B rides in a circular guide. B therefore moves on a circle of radius 0.5 m, exactly like the tip of an imaginary crank pinned at the guide's centre:
  • $\vec v_B$ is tangent to the guide (⊥ to the radius).
  • $\vec a_B$ has a normal part $v_B^2/0.5$ pointing at the guide's centre, plus an unknown tangential part along the guide.
AB is rigid. Seen from A, B can only swing around A, so:
  • $\vec v_{B/A}$ is ⊥ AB.
  • $(\vec a_{B/A})_n = \omega_{AB}^2 AB$ points from B to A, and $(\vec a_{B/A})_t$ is ⊥ AB.
That's every direction you need. The rest is geometry and drawing.

Step 1 · Geometry: the angle of AB

{{fig:ct2_geom}}
The guide's centre is 0.5 m above the track (that's how the circle meets the track smoothly). B sits on the 60° radius, so it is $h_1 = 0.5\cos 60° = 0.25$ m below the centre.
B is therefore $h_2 = 0.5 - 0.25 = 0.25$ m above the track, which is where A is.
The bar is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with height 0.25 m and length 1.2 m: $\;\beta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{0.25}{1.2} = 12.0°$ below the horizontal (from B down to A).

How to draw it (scale 1 cm = 1 m/s)

  1. From the pole o, draw $\vec v_A$: 3 cm to the right, ending at a.
  2. Through a, draw a long line at 258° (the ⊥-AB direction).
  3. Through o, draw a long line at 300° (the tangent direction).
  4. They cross at b. Measure ob = $v_B$ and ab = $v_{B/A}$.
Angles in the triangle (to use the sine rule instead of measuring): at o, the angle between 0° and 300° is 60°; at a, it's between 180° and 258°, so 78°; at b, 180 − 60 − 78 = 42°.
$v_B = \dfrac{3\sin 78°}{\sin 42°} = 4.38$ m/s @ 300° (down-right along the guide)
$v_{B/A} = \dfrac{3\sin 60°}{\sin 42°} = 3.88$ m/s @ 258°
$\omega_{AB} = \dfrac{v_{B/A}}{AB} = \dfrac{3.88}{1.2} = 3.23$ rad/s, CCW

[Sense: B is up-left of A, and $\vec v_{B/A}$ points down (258°). That swings B anticlockwise around A. B also travels round its guide at $\omega_{OB} = v_B/0.5 = 8.77$ rad/s, which you'll need for its normal acceleration.]

Check with the instantaneous centre

Draw the line ⊥ $\vec v_A$ (the vertical through A) and the line ⊥ $\vec v_B$ (the radius through B, extended). They meet at the IC, 0.928 m above A.
$\omega_{AB} = v_A/0.928 = 3.23$ rad/s ✓; $\;v_B = 3.23 \times 1.355 = 4.38$ m/s ✓
A is directly below the IC and moves right, which confirms the rotation is CCW.

Step 3 · (b) Accelerations

Equation: $\vec a_B = \vec a_A + \vec a_{B/A}$. Split each side into the parts whose directions you know:

{{fig:ct2_acc}}
Read off: $(a_B)_t = 23.9$ m/s² @ 120° and $(a_{B/A})_t = 43.5$ m/s² @ 78°
$\alpha_{AB} = \dfrac{(a_{B/A})_t}{AB} = \dfrac{43.5}{1.2} = 36.2$ rad/s², CW

[What the senses mean: $(\vec a_{B/A})_t$ points up (78°) at B, which is up-left of A, so it turns AB clockwise. AB spins CCW but with a CW α, so its rotation is slowing down. $(\vec a_B)_t$ at 120° is opposite to $\vec v_B$ at 300°, so B is slowing down along the guide too. The total: $a_B = \sqrt{38.4^2 + 23.9^2} = 45.2$ m/s².]

Where the marks are, and the traps

  • Geometry: h₁ and β, with a sketch.
  • Velocity: the equation, a scaled diagram with the scale stated, vA drawn correctly, then vB and vB/A with their directions.
  • Acceleration: the full expanded equation, the unknown directions marked, aA = 0, both normal terms calculated and drawn correctly, the scale, both tangential terms with their angles, then α with its sense.
  • Trap 1: forgetting $(\vec a_B)_n$. B is on a curve, so it always has a normal acceleration towards the guide's centre.
  • Trap 2: drawing $(\vec a_{B/A})_n$ the wrong way. It points from B to A (towards the reference point).
  • Trap 3: giving a value with no vector diagram. The memo says those get no marks.
