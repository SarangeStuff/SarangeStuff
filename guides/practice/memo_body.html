Question 1 [12]

(a) Same rigid body (situation 1)

(i) $v_{B/A} = \omega\,AB = 4(0.25) = 1$ m/s {{t}}, ⊥ AB. Rotating 30° clockwise by 90° gives 300° (down-right) {{t}}
(ii) $\vec v_{A/B} = -\vec v_{B/A}$: 1 m/s at 120° {{t}}
(iii) $(a_{B/A})_n = \omega^2 AB = 16(0.25) = 4$ m/s² {{t}}, from B towards A: 210° {{t}}

[A common slip: drawing $\vec v_{B/A}$ along AB. A rigid link can't stretch, so B can't move towards or away from A.]

(b) Separate bodies (situation 2)

No, the ⊥ rule doesn't apply: the cars aren't joined, so nothing stops the gap between them changing {{t}}
$\vec v_{B/A} = \vec v_B - \vec v_A = (0, 10) - (24, 0) = (-24,\ 10)$ m/s {{t}}
$|\vec v_{B/A}| = \sqrt{24^2 + 10^2} = 26$ m/s {{t}}, at $\tan^{-1}(10/24) = 22.6°$ north of west {{t}}

(c) Touching, no slip (situation 4)

The tangential acceleration is shared: $(a_{A'})_t = 100$ m/s² {{t}}
$\alpha_B = 100/2 = 50$ rad/s² {{t}}; $\;\alpha_C = 100/1 = 100$ rad/s², in the opposite sense {{t}}

[Only the tangential parts match. The normal parts $r\omega^2$ differ, because the gears have different radii and different ω.]
