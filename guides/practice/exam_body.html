Theory sheet · Which relative-velocity rule applies?

{{fig:situations}}
SituationWhat you writeDirection of the relative termHow to picture it
1 · Same rigid body
(two points on one link, bar, plate or wheel)		$\vec v_B = \vec v_A + \vec v_{B/A}$, with $v_{B/A} = \omega\,AB$⊥ to the line AB. Its sense (which way along the ⊥) comes from ω, or from closing the triangle.
Acceleration: $(a_{B/A})_n$ along BA, pointing to A; $(a_{B/A})_t$ ⊥ AB.		Stand on A. The body can't stretch, so B can only swing round you in a circle.
2 · Separate bodies
(cars, boats, planes, two particles)		$\vec v_{B/A} = \vec v_B - \vec v_A$Any direction. No ⊥ rule: nothing ties A to B.Ride in A. The whole world drifts backwards at $\vec v_A$; B's apparent motion is its own motion plus that drift. Draw both absolute arrows from one point o: $\vec v_{B/A}$ runs from the tip of $\vec v_A$ to the tip of $\vec v_B$.
3 · Different bodies joined by a pin
(crank + rod, four-bar)		Chain through the pin: $\vec v_B$ from the first link, then $\vec v_C = \vec v_B + \vec v_{C/B}$ on the nextUse rule 1 inside each link, with that link's own ω.The pin is the "hand-over" point: one velocity belonging to both links.
4 · Bodies touching
(gears, belts, rolling, rollers on plates)		No slip (meshing, rolling): the contact points share their whole velocity and their tangential acceleration.
Sliding (a roller on a plate): they share only the component ⊥ to the contact surface.		Normal accelerations differ between bodies, because each has its own $r\omega^2$.Ask: can they slip past each other here? Whatever direction they can slide along is not shared.

Golden habit: before writing any equation, say out loud which of the four situations you're in.

SECTION A · Relative motion [62 marks]

Question 1 [12 marks] · Direction drill

(a) Link AB is 250 mm long, with B up and to the right of A at 30° above the horizontal. It turns at ω = 4 rad/s clockwise.

  1. Find the size and direction (angle from +x) of $\vec v_{B/A}$. (2)
  2. Write down $\vec v_{A/B}$. (1)
  3. Find the size and direction of $(\vec a_{B/A})_n$. (2)

(b) Car A drives east at 24 m/s and car B drives north at 10 m/s on a crossing road. Is $\vec v_{B/A}$ perpendicular to the line joining the cars? Explain in one line, then find $\vec v_{B/A}$ (size and direction). (4)

(c) Gear B (radius 2 m) meshes with gear C (radius 1 m) at point A. The tangential acceleration of A on gear B is 100 m/s². Find the tangential acceleration of the touching point A′ on gear C, and the angular accelerations of both gears. (3)

Theory hint (a) is situation 1, (b) is situation 2, (c) is situation 4 (no slip).
  1. (a) Rotate the line AB by 90° in the sense of ω.
  2. (a)(iii) Normal parts always point towards the reference point.
  3. (b) Subtract the vectors in components; draw the triangle from one point o.
  4. (c) What's shared at a no-slip contact: the whole $a$, or only part of it?

Question 2 [14 marks] · Two cars on a circular track (variant of CT1 2025 Q2: new numbers)

Car A is on the outer lane (radius 375 m) at 25° above the horizontal line through the centre, travelling at a constant 90 km/h. Car B is on the inner lane (radius 360 m) on that horizontal line, travelling at 72 km/h and speeding up at 0.8 m/s². Both cars move anticlockwise round the track.

  1. Draw a scaled velocity diagram and find the magnitude of $\vec v_{A/B}$. (6)
  2. Draw a scaled acceleration diagram and find the magnitude and direction of $\vec a_{A/B}$. (8)
Theory hint Separate bodies (situation 2): no ⊥ rule. $\vec v_{A/B} = \vec v_A - \vec v_B$ joins the tip of $\vec v_B$ to the tip of $\vec v_A$. Each car's acceleration is $v^2/\rho$ towards the centre, plus $\dot v$ along its motion.
  1. Convert km/h to m/s.
  2. Each velocity is ⊥ to its car's radius line.
  3. Constant speed means no tangential part.
  4. Draw each absolute vector from the same point o, then close the gap.

Question 3 [16 marks] · Homework 5/119: one answer, two routes

{{crop:p5119}}
  1. Find the velocity of collar A using instantaneous centres. (10)
  2. Find it again using $\vec v_A = \vec v_B + \vec v_{A/B}$, and show the two routes agree, including ωAB. (6)
Theory hint Two links joined at pin B (situation 3). Get $\vec v_B$ from link OBD, which rotates about the fixed O. Then hand B's velocity to link AB, where the same-body rule applies.
  1. D's velocity is ⊥ OD, and you're only given its vertical component.
  2. Find B, then A, with trig: A is on the shaft and AB = 700 mm.
  3. IC of AB: draw ⊥ $\vec v_A$ through A and ⊥ $\vec v_B$ through B. One of these is simply line OB, extended.
  4. Relative route: $\vec v_{A/B}$ is ⊥ AB. Find AB's angle first.

Question 5 [10 marks] · Homework 5/120: gears touching

{{crop:p5120}}
Theory hint Meshing gears share the velocity of their contact point (situation 4, no slip). Across one gear, velocity varies linearly: the same velocity line as the pulleys.

SECTION B · Rotation, absolute motion, and the link to kinetics [38 marks]

Question 6 [20 marks] · Tutorial 11 Q4: relative acceleration feeding ΣF = ma

{{crop:t11q4}}
  1. Find ωAB and ωCB. (4)
  2. Draw the acceleration polygon. Find αAB, αCB and the acceleration of G, the centre of AB. (8)
  3. Draw the FBD and KD of AB and find the force in CB. (8)
Theory hint Kinetics needs $\vec a_G$ and α, and the acceleration polygon is where they come from. Velocities → accelerations → $\vec a_G$ → FBD/KD → moments.
  1. Both A and B move horizontally at this instant. What does that say about ωAB?
  2. Pick + directions for ωOA and αOA from the arrows, then find both parts of $\vec a_A$.
  3. G is the midpoint of AB, so g is the midpoint of ab on your polygon.
  4. CB is massless: its force acts along CB. Take moments about A to remove Ax and Ay.

Question 7 [18 marks] · Rotation (homework 5/17) and absolute motion (homework 5/44)

(a) Homework 5/17: find the velocity and acceleration of A, in vector form and as magnitudes. (10)

{{crop:p517}}

(b) Homework 5/44: derive ω of lever OA in terms of x, then evaluate it for r = 200 mm, x = 500 mm and v = 1.5 m/s. State CW or CCW. (8)

{{crop:p544}}
Theory hint (a) Fixed axis: $\vec v = \vec\omega\times\vec r$ and $\vec a = \vec\alpha\times\vec r - \omega^2\vec r$. Read the ω and α arrows very carefully. (b) Geometry, then differentiate: the lever is tangent to the disk.
  1. (a) OA comes straight from the cosine rule, using the 120° angle.
  2. (a) Shortcut: $\omega\vec k\times(x\vec i + y\vec j) = \omega(-y\vec i + x\vec j)$.
  3. (b) Draw the right triangle: centre to O is x, and centre to the lever is r. Is x growing or shrinking?
