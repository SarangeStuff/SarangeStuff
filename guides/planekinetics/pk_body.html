What changes once forces get involved: the moment of inertia, the kinetic diagram, choosing the moment point, and whether the body has an angular acceleration at all. Then every tutorial question, solved the memo way and with vectors, so you can be sure your answer isn't a fluke.

Part 1 · The ideas

1Three diagrams, three jobs

Most mix-ups come from putting the right arrow on the wrong diagram. Each diagram answers one question:

FBD = KD means the two systems are equivalent: the same total push ($\sum\vec F = m\vec a_G$) and the same total twist about any point you choose. Velocities never appear on either. They only enter through ω (the normal terms $r\omega^2$) and through the direction a body is slipping, which sets friction's direction.

2The equations (from your formula sheet)

$\sum\vec F = m\vec a_G$ — always, for every body.

$\sum M_G = I_G\alpha$ — moments about the mass centre.

$\sum M_O = I_O\alpha$ — about a fixed pivot O only.

$\sum M_P = I_G\alpha + m a_G d$ — about any point P, where d is the ⊥ distance from P to the line of $m\vec a_G$.

Read the last one as: moments of the FBD about P = moments of the KD about P. The KD has two things that can twist about P: the curl $I_G\alpha$ (the same about every point) and the arrow $m\vec a_G$ (which has a moment unless it passes through P).

Vector form of the same thing: $\sum(\vec r_{i/P}\times\vec F_i) = I_G\vec\alpha + \vec r_{G/P}\times m\vec a_G$, with $(x\vec i + y\vec j)\times(F_x\vec i + F_y\vec j) = (xF_y - yF_x)\vec k$.

3Where I and the parallel-axis theorem come in

I is rotational mass. $m$ resists $\vec a_G$; $I_G$ resists α. It depends on how far the mass sits from the axis (∑ m r²), which is why a body's shape matters.

Why $I_O = I_G + md^2$ is not just a rule. Take a body pinned at a fixed O, with G at distance r. Its KD has three items:

  • $I_G\alpha$ → moment $I_G\alpha$ about O
  • $m(a_G)_t = m r\alpha$, ⊥ OG → moment $(mr\alpha)\,r$ about O
  • $m(a_G)_n = m r\omega^2$, along GO → passes through O → no moment

Add them: $\sum M_O = I_G\alpha + mr^2\alpha = (I_G + mr^2)\alpha = I_O\alpha$.

So the parallel-axis theorem is the moment of the $m\,r\alpha$ arrow, folded into one number. Use $\sum M_O = I_O\alpha$ only where that derivation holds: a fixed O (or G itself).

How this differs from plain rotation in kinematics

  • In kinematics you were given ω and α and found velocities and accelerations. In kinetics α is an unknown that the moments produce, so you need I.
  • A body moving on a circular path isn't necessarily rotating. The T10 Q1 pipe travels on an arc, but it translates.
  • "Released from rest" means ω = 0, not α = 0. ω = 0 kills the normal terms ($r\omega^2$); α is exactly what you're solving for.
  • ω can be zero while α isn't (T11 Q4: ωAB = 0, αAB = 0.667 rad/s²).

5Choosing the moment point

  1. Fixed pivot O? Take moments about O: the pin reactions vanish and $\sum M_O = I_O\alpha$ gives α in one line (T10 Q2, Q3, T11 Q1).
  2. Several unknown forces meet at one point (a contact point carrying F and N, or a pin carrying Ax and Ay)? Take moments there, but use the full $\sum M_P = I_G\alpha + m a_G d$ (T10 Q1 about A, T11 Q3 about C, T11 Q4 about A).
  3. Otherwise use G. $\sum M_G = I_G\alpha$ is always valid and always safe (T10 Q4, Q5).
  4. Count unknowns: in the plane you get 3 equations ($\sum F_x$, $\sum F_y$, one moment equation) plus the kinematic links.

The one illegal move

  • $\sum M_P = I_P\alpha$ about an accelerating point P is wrong in general, because it drops the $m a_G d$ term. (The exception you'll meet is a round body rolling without slip, about its contact point.)

7Drawing to scale without fluking it

  1. Compute every angle first with trig (tan⁻¹ from the dimensions) and write it on the sketch. Never eyeball angles.
  2. Choose a scale that makes the biggest vector 5–10 cm long, and write it down (e.g. 1 cm = 0.1 m/s²).
  3. Draw the fully known vectors first, measured with a ruler and placed with a protractor at the computed angle.
  4. For unknown sizes, draw the direction line only (⊥ to the link, along the guide), long and thin.
  5. The crossing closes the diagram. Measure it, then convert with the scale.
  6. Confirm with vectors: write each arrow in i, j components and check that the sums close. If they don't, your drawing has an angle wrong.

8The master recipe

  1. Classify the motion (card 4). That decides whether α exists and which moment equation is legal.
  2. Kinematics: velocities first (for ω), then $\vec a_G$ in terms of α.
  3. FBD (forces) and KD ($m\vec a_G$, $I_G\alpha$) side by side, same geometry.
  4. Moment point (card 5) and I (card 6).
  5. Equations: $\sum F_x$, $\sum F_y$, one moment equation, plus the kinematic link.
  6. Solve and state directions (CW/CCW, ↑↓←→).
  7. Vector check: redo it in components.
  8. Sanity check: assumptions (no slip: F ≤ μN), signs, sizes.

Geometry (TRUE SCALE)

{{fig:w1_geom}}

Moment arms: the horizontal 2T acts at height 16 sin 30° = 8 m above O. The weight acts at horizontal distance 12 cos 30° = 10.39 m from O.

Scalar (memo route)

  1. Tension: $\tau = T r_{drum}$, so $T = 1300/0.6 = 2167$ N and the cable at A carries $2T = 4333$ N.

  7. (↑ +) $\;O_y - mg = 300(0.850\sin 60°)$, so $O_y = 3.16$ kN

$\vec r_{G/O} = 12(\cos 30°, \sin 30°) = (10.39,\ 6)$ m

$\vec a_G = \alpha\vec k\times\vec r_{G/O} - \omega^2\vec r = 0.0709(-6,\ 10.39) = (-0.425,\ 0.737)$ m/s²

$\sum M_O$: $\;(0, 8)\times(-4333, 0) + (10.39, 6)\times(0, -2943)$

Geometry (TRUE SCALE)

{{fig:w2_geom}}

G is at (b, −b/2) from O:

W4Tutorial 10 Q1: semicircular pipe on a swinging platform

Curvilinear TRANSLATIONpipe: α = 0, ω = 0I: not neededMoments about A
{{crop:t10q1}}

80 kg semicircular pipe (radius 500 mm, ends A and B 1 m apart) on a platform carried by two equal 1 m links at θ = 45°, μs = 0.3. Starting from rest, find the largest α of the links with no slip, and NB.

{{fig:w4_mech}}

The two equal parallel links make a parallelogram, so the platform never turns: it translates on a circular path. If the pipe doesn't slip, it translates with it. The links have α; the pipe has α = 0.

From rest, ω = 0, so each link tip has only $a_t = r\alpha$, ⊥ to the link: up and to the left at 45°. The pipe has exactly this acceleration.

{{fig:w4_acc}}

Scalar (memo route)

  1. (← +) $\;F_A + F_B = m r\alpha\sin 45°$
  2. (↑ +) $\;N_A + N_B - mg = m r\alpha\cos 45°$
  3. About to slip: $F_A + F_B = 0.3(N_A + N_B)$. Combine:
    $0.3mg = mr\alpha(\sin 45° - 0.3\cos 45°)$
  4. $\alpha = \dfrac{0.3(9.81)}{1(0.7071 - 0.2121)} = 5.95$ rad/s² CCW
  5. (↺ +) $\sum M_A = m a_G d$ (no $I_G\alpha$ term because α = 0). The friction forces lie on the line through A, so they have no moment:
    $N_B(1) - mg(0.5) = m(r\alpha)(0.5786)$
  6. $N_B = 392.4 + 80(5.946)(0.5786) = 668$ N

Where d = 0.5786 m comes from

$\vec r_{G/A} = (0.5,\ 0.318)$; the line of $m\vec a$ points along $(-0.7071,\ 0.7071)$.

$d = 0.5(0.7071) - 0.318(-0.7071) = 0.5786$ m. The memo's version: AG is at 32.5° from the horizontal and $|AG| = 0.593$ m, so $d = 0.593\sin(135° - 32.5°)$.

Vector check

$\vec a = 5.946(\cos 135°, \sin 135°) = (-4.20,\ 4.20)$

$\sum F_x$: $F = 80(4.20) = 336.3$ N; $\;\sum F_y$: $N_A + N_B = 784.8 + 336.3 = 1121.1$ N; $\;0.3 \times 1121.1 = 336.3$ ✓

With $N_B = 667.6$ and $N_A = 453.5$, the moments of all forces about G sum to zero, as they must when α = 0 ✓

α = 5.95 rad/s² CCW (links) · NB = 668 N

W6Tutorial 10 Q5: bowling ball that slips, then rolls

General plane motion WITH slipaG and α independentI: $I_G = mk^2$ (k given)Moments about G
{{crop:t10q5}}

6.4 kg ball, r = 120 mm, k = 83 mm, released at 6 m/s with ω = 0, μ = 0.2. Find the distance it slides before it rolls without slipping.

While slipping, the contact point slides forward ($v_C = v - r\omega > 0$), so friction acts backwards with its full value $F = \mu N$. It slows G and spins the ball up. Because the ball slips, don't use $a_G = r\alpha$.

{{fig:w6_graph}}

Slipping ends where the two lines meet: t = 0.990 s, v = rω = 4.06 m/s.

Scalar (memo route)

  1. (↑ +) $N = mg$; (→ +) $-\mu N = ma_G$, so $a_G = \mu g = 1.962$ m/s² (slowing)
  2. (↻ +) $\sum M_G = I_G\alpha$: $\;\mu mg\,r = mk^2\alpha \Rightarrow \alpha = \dfrac{\mu g r}{k^2} = 34.2$ rad/s²
  3. $v = v_0 - \mu g\,t$ and $\omega = \alpha t$
  4. Rolling starts when $v = r\omega$: $\;6 - 1.962t = 0.12(34.18)t$, so $t = 0.990$ s and $v = 4.06$ m/s
    (the memo's form: $v = v_0/(1 + k^2/r^2)$)
  5. $s = \dfrac{v_0^2 - v^2}{2\mu g} = \dfrac{36 - 16.47}{3.924} = 4.98$ m

Why this I?

A real bowling ball has finger holes and a dense core, so it isn't a uniform sphere. The question gives k, so $I_G = mk^2 = 6.4(0.083)^2 = 0.0441$ kg·m².

Using the sphere formula $\tfrac25 mr^2 = 0.0369$ kg·m² would give s = 4.49 m, which is wrong. Always use the k you're given.

Check

$s = v_0t - \tfrac12\mu g t^2 = 6(0.9896) - \tfrac12(1.962)(0.9896)^2 = 4.98$ m ✓

s = 4.98 m (v = 4.06 m/s when rolling begins)

Rolling without slip: the hoop centre has $a_O = l\alpha$ →. Relative: $a_{G/O} = (l/2)\alpha$ ↓ (tangential only, because ω = 0). So $\vec a_G = (l\alpha,\ -\tfrac{l}{2}\alpha)$.

Shortcut: moments about C

F and N act at C, so they drop out. Only the weight has a moment (arm l/2). On the KD, every arrow has a moment:

(↻ +) $\;mg\tfrac{l}{2} = I_G\alpha + m(l\alpha)(l) + m(\tfrac{l}{2}\alpha)(\tfrac{l}{2})$

$\tfrac{g}{2} = l\alpha\left(\tfrac{1}{12} + 1 + \tfrac14\right) = \tfrac43 l\alpha \Rightarrow \alpha = \dfrac{3g}{8l}$ ✓
