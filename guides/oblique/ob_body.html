MEC2047S Dynamics · Oblique impact, slowly

The n–t idea, from zero

You already know direct (head-on) impact: momentum plus restitution. Oblique impact is that same head-on impact, done along one tilted direction, while the other direction does nothing at all. This doc builds the tilted directions up from scratch, then walks through S25 Q6 and F25 Q8 one small step at a time.

Part 1 · What n and t actually are

1Two directions, nothing more

n and t are just a pair of directions, like x and y, but tilted to suit the collision:

  • n (for "normal"): the line through both centres. That's the direction the balls push each other.
  • t (for "tangent"): 90° to n, along the surfaces where they touch.

That's it: no new physics. "n–t coordinates" just means measuring every velocity along these two tilted directions instead of along x and y. Each velocity gets an "n part" and a "t part", its components.

Why bother tilting? Because when two smooth balls hit, the push between them acts only along n. So the n direction is where all the action is, and the t direction is boring: nothing happens there.

{{fig:idea}}

2The whole method in two sentences

Along t: nothing pushes, so each ball keeps its own t-speed. Copy it across.
Along n: it's an ordinary head-on impact: momentum conserved, plus e. Use exactly the equations you already know, but with n-parts only.

Then put each ball's n and t parts back together to get its final speed and direction. Everything below is just practice at that.

3Splitting an arrow into its n and t parts (components)

Imagine shining a light straight down onto each axis: the arrow's "shadow" on that axis is its component.

  1. Find φ, the angle between the velocity and the n axis.
  2. n part = $v\cos\varphi$ (cos goes with the axis the angle is measured from).
  3. t part = $v\sin\varphi$ (the other one).
  4. Signs: a part that points the opposite way to your chosen +n or +t gets a minus.

Even easier with a horizontal velocity (both exam questions use one): if n is tilted θ from the vertical, a horizontal speed v splits as

n part $= v\sin\theta$, $\;$ t part $= v\cos\theta$

Sanity test: if θ were 0 (n vertical), a horizontal velocity would have no n part at all, and sin 0 = 0 ✓. If θ were 90° (n horizontal), it would be all n part, and sin 90° = 1 ✓.

The vector way gives the same thing: n part $= \vec v\cdot\hat n$ and t part $= \vec v\cdot\hat t$, where $\hat n$ and $\hat t$ are the tilted unit arrows in x–y form.

4Warm-up: a ball bouncing off a wall

A ball hits a smooth wall at 10 m/s, at 30° to the wall, with e = 0.6.

  1. n is ⊥ to the wall; t runs along it.
  2. Split: t part $= 10\cos 30° = 8.66$ m/s (along the wall); n part $= 10\sin 30° = 5$ m/s (into the wall).
  3. t: the wall can't push along itself, so the t part stays 8.66.
  4. n: it bounces back with $e \times 5 = 3$ m/s, now away from the wall.
  5. Rebuild: speed $= \sqrt{8.66^2 + 3^2} = 9.17$ m/s, at $\tan^{-1}(3/8.66) = 19.1°$ to the wall.

See the pattern? t untouched, n reversed and shrunk. Two balls work the same way, except that along n you share momentum between them.

{{fig:wall}}

5The recipe (use it every time)

  1. Draw n through both centres and t at 90° to it. Mark which way is + for each. Find the angle of n from the figure.
  2. Make a table: each body, its n part and its t part before the impact (with signs).
  3. t: copy each free, smooth body's t part across unchanged.
  4. n: two equations, two unknowns (the two n parts after):
    • momentum: $m_A(v_A)_n + m_B(v_B)_n = m_A(v'_A)_n + m_B(v'_B)_n$
    • restitution: $e = \dfrac{\text{speed apart after}}{\text{speed together before}}$, both measured along n.
  5. Rebuild: speed $= \sqrt{(\text{n part})^2 + (\text{t part})^2}$; direction from $\tan^{-1}$.
  6. Check: kinetic energy after ≤ before.

Part 2 · S25 Q6: two smooth disks (free bodies)

QThe question

Two smooth disks A (8 kg) and B (6 kg) collide at O. Just before impact, A moves left at 5 m/s and B moves right at 7 m/s. The line through their centres has a slope marked by a 5–12–13 triangle, and e = 0.5. Find their speeds just after impact. [10 marks]

1Draw n and t, and find the angle

  • n = the line through both centres. The 5–12–13 triangle says it rises 12 for every 5 across.
  • Measured from the vertical: $\tan\theta = 5/12$, so $\theta = 22.6°$.
  • Exact helpers, no calculator needed: $\sin\theta = 5/13 = 0.3846$ and $\cos\theta = 12/13 = 0.9231$.
  • Choose +n = up-right (from B towards A) and +t = down-right (⊥ to n).
{{fig:s25_axes}}

2Split both velocities (the "before" table)

Both velocities are horizontal and n is tilted θ from the vertical, so: n part $= v\sin\theta$, t part $= v\cos\theta$, with signs from the picture.

n part (↗ +)t part (↘ +)
A: 5 m/s ←$-5(5/13) = -1.923$$-5(12/13) = -4.615$
B: 7 m/s →$+7(5/13) = +2.692$$+7(12/13) = +6.462$

Why the minus signs for A? A moves left, and both +n and +t lean right, so both of A's parts point "backwards".

{{fig:s25_split}}

3t: copy across

Smooth disks can't push each other along t, so $(v'_A)_t = -4.615$ m/s and $(v'_B)_t = +6.462$ m/s. Done.

4n: a normal head-on impact

Momentum along n:

$8(-1.923) + 6(2.692) = 8(v'_A)_n + 6(v'_B)_n$

$0.769 = 8(v'_A)_n + 6(v'_B)_n$ ……(1)

Restitution along n: before, B (below) closes on A (above) at $2.692 - (-1.923) = 4.615$ m/s. After, they separate at e times that: $0.5 \times 4.615 = 2.308$ m/s, with A ahead in +n:

$(v'_A)_n - (v'_B)_n = 2.308$ ……(2)

Solve: from (2), $(v'_A)_n = (v'_B)_n + 2.308$. Into (1):

$8(v'_B)_n + 18.46 + 6(v'_B)_n = 0.769$

$(v'_B)_n = -1.264$ m/s, $\;(v'_A)_n = +1.044$ m/s

Same answer from the memo's form: $e = \dfrac{(v'_B)_n - (v'_A)_n}{(v_A)_n - (v_B)_n} = \dfrac{-1.264 - 1.044}{-1.923 - 2.692} = 0.5$ ✓. It's the same idea: the gap closing before versus the gap opening after.

5Rebuild each velocity

n partt partspeed
A after+1.044−4.615$\sqrt{1.044^2 + 4.615^2} = $ 4.73 m/s
B after−1.264+6.462$\sqrt{1.264^2 + 6.462^2} = $ 6.58 m/s

The question asks for speeds, so you're done. If it asked for directions: A now heads up-left at 35.4° above the horizontal, and B heads down-right at 33.7° below it.

Energy check: before $= \tfrac12(8)(5^2) + \tfrac12(6)(7^2) = 247$ J; after $= 219.6$ J. That's less, as it must be with e < 1 ✓

Part 3 · F25 Q8: bat and ball (the bat is stuck in a slot)

QThe question

The bat A (0.5 kg) slides to the right at 18 m/s inside a horizontal slot. The ball B (0.146 kg) comes in from the right at 38 m/s. The line through their centres is at 20° above the horizontal, and e = 0.5. Find the ball's speed after impact and the angle β of its velocity to the horizontal. [14 marks]

{{fig:f25_axes}}

!The one twist: the slot

The slot holds the bat. During the hit, the slot pushes the bat up or down, so there's an outside push on the system. Because that push has a piece along n, momentum along n is NOT conserved this time.
The slot can't push sideways (horizontally), so conserve momentum horizontally instead.
  • The ball is still free and smooth, so its t part is still unchanged.
  • Restitution still works along n: it's about the contact between the two bodies, not about outside pushes.
  • The bat can only move horizontally, so its unknown is just one number: $v'_A$.

1Axes and the "before" table

+n = along the line of centres, 20° above the horizontal, pointing from A towards B. +t = 90° further round (up-left).

n partt part
A: 18 m/s →$18\cos 20° = +16.91$(not needed: the slot changes it)
B: 38 m/s ←$-38\cos 20° = -35.71$$+38\sin 20° = +13.00$

The ball moves left, almost straight against +n (so a big negative n part), and a little along +t (up-left).

2Three equations, three unknowns

Unknowns: the bat's $v'_A$ and the ball's $(v'_B)_n$. The ball's t part is already known.

(i) Ball's t part unchanged: $(v'_B)_t = 13.00$ m/s

(ii) Horizontal momentum (→ +):

$0.5(18) + 0.146(-38) = 0.5\,v'_A + 0.146\,(v'_B)_x$

with $(v'_B)_x = (v'_B)_n\cos 20° - 13.00\sin 20°$ (the ball's n and t parts each have a horizontal share)

(iii) Restitution along n: the gap closes at $16.91 - (-35.71) = 52.62$ m/s, and opens at $0.5 \times 52.62 = 26.31$ m/s:

$(v'_B)_n - v'_A\cos 20° = 26.31$

Solve (ii) and (iii) together:

$v'_A = 0.781$ m/s → (the bat almost stops)

$(v'_B)_n = 27.05$ m/s

Rebuild the ball:

speed $= \sqrt{27.05^2 + 13.00^2} = $ 30.0 m/s

In x–y: $(v'_B)_x = 20.97$ and $(v'_B)_y = 21.46$, so $\beta = \tan^{-1}(21.46/20.97) = $ 45.7° above the horizontal, up and to the right.

If you (wrongly) conserved momentum along n as if the bat were free, you'd get 28.5 m/s. Close enough to look right, but it's wrong.

{{fig:f25_after}}

v′B = 30.0 m/s at β = 45.7° (bat: 0.781 m/s →)

✓Mistakes to avoid

  • e only ever lives along n. Never use x or y velocities in the e equation.
  • t is unchanged per ball, not for the system, and only for a body nothing else is holding.
  • Free bodies: conserve momentum along n. A body held in a slot or guide: conserve momentum only in the direction the guide can't push.
  • sin or cos? Test an extreme angle: if n were along the velocity, the whole velocity would be its n part.
  • Signs: write the + arrows for n and t on your sketch before you split anything.
  • Speed or velocity? Speed is just the size, so the sign doesn't matter. Velocity needs a direction (β).
