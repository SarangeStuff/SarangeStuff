Rigid bodies: spinning about a fixed axis

In this chapter
  • The three kinds of planar motion
  • Angular position, velocity and acceleration
  • The three formulas for any point on a spinning body: \(v=\omega r\), \(a_n=\omega^2 r\), \(a_t=\alpha r\)
  • Gears and belts

From here on, objects have size. A rigid body never changes shape: the distance between any two of its points stays the same. That one property is what makes everything in Chapters 6 to 10 work.

Three kinds of planar motion

  • Translation: every line on the body stays parallel to itself. It can be rectilinear (straight paths) or curvilinear (curved paths). Either way, every point has the same velocity and acceleration, so the body behaves like one particle.
  • Rotation about a fixed axis: every point moves on a circle round the axis (a crank, a gear, a door).
  • General plane motion: translation and rotation at once (a connecting rod, a rolling wheel, a sliding ladder, a thrown Frisbee). This is where Chapters 7 to 10 live.

Angular motion is Chapter 2 again

Replace \(s, v, a\) by \(\theta, \omega, \alpha\) and every rectilinear result carries straight over:

\[ \omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt} \qquad \alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt} \qquad \omega\,d\omega = \alpha\,d\theta \]
\[ \text{constant }\alpha:\quad \omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t \qquad \Delta\theta = \omega_0 t + \tfrac12\alpha t^2 \qquad \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\,\Delta\theta \]

Anticlockwise is positive. Use the same decision table as in Chapter 2: ask whether \(\alpha\) is given as a function of \(t\), \(\omega\) or \(\theta\). (The 2019 exam pulley question gives \(\alpha = 0.8\theta\), so it needs \(\omega\,d\omega = \alpha\,d\theta\).)

A point on a spinning body

\[ v = \omega r \quad(\perp OP) \qquad\qquad a_n = \omega^2 r \quad(\text{towards }O) \qquad\qquad a_t = \alpha r \quad(\perp OP) \]
Why these three

P moves on a circle of radius \(r\), so this is just n–t motion (Chapter 3) with \(\rho = r\).

  • In one radian of turn, P travels a distance \(r\). Turning at \(\omega\) rad/s, it travels \(\omega r\) metres per second.
  • \(a_n = v^2/\rho = (\omega r)^2/r = \omega^2 r\). It always points to the centre, because the velocity is being turned towards it.
  • \(a_t = \dot v = \frac{d}{dt}(\omega r) = \alpha r\), because \(r\) is fixed.

In vector form these are \(\vec v = \vec\omega\times\vec r\), \(\vec a_n = \vec\omega\times(\vec\omega\times\vec r) = -\omega^2\vec r\) and \(\vec a_t = \vec\alpha\times\vec r\). These are the Chapter 1 cross product turning \(\vec r\) a quarter-turn.

Common slip

If a crank turns at constant \(\omega\), then \(\alpha = 0\) and \(a_t = 0\), but \(a_n = \omega^2 r\) is not zero. A point moving at steady speed round a circle is still accelerating, towards the centre. Forgetting this costs marks in every relative acceleration question.

Gears and belts

Where two wheels touch without slipping, or are joined by a belt, the touching points share the same speed and the same tangential acceleration:

\[ \omega_A r_A = \omega_B r_B \qquad\qquad \alpha_A r_A = \alpha_B r_B \]

Their normal accelerations differ, because \(\omega^2 r\) depends on each wheel’s own \(\omega\) and \(r\) (L17, “bodies in contact”).

Worked example 6.1: belt drive starting from rest

Like L11 Example 1 and Tut03 Q4

Pulley A (radius 50 mm) starts from rest with \(\alpha_A = 6t\) rad/s². A belt drives pulley B (radius 150 mm). At \(t = 2\) s, find \(\omega_B\), the angle B has turned, and the acceleration of a point on B’s rim.

  1. \(\alpha\) depends on \(t\), so integrate: \(\omega_A = \int_0^t 6t\,dt = 3t^2 = 12\) rad/s at \(t=2\), and \(\theta_A = \int_0^t 3t^2\,dt = t^3 = 8\) rad.
  2. The belt links the rims: \(\omega_B = \omega_A r_A/r_B = 12\times50/150 = 4.00\) rad/s, and in the same ratio \(\theta_B = 8/3 = 2.67\) rad and \(\alpha_B = 12/3 = 4\) rad/s².The belt does not stretch or slip, so both rims move the same distance, at the same speed, with the same tangential acceleration.
  3. Rim of B: \(a_t = \alpha_B r_B = 4(0.15) = 0.60\) m/s² and \(a_n = \omega_B^2 r_B = 16(0.15) = 2.40\) m/s², so \(a = \sqrt{0.6^2 + 2.4^2}\).

ωB = 4.00 rad/s, θB = 2.67 rad, a = 2.47 m/s²

In one breath
  • Rigid means fixed distances between points.
  • \(\theta, \omega, \alpha\) follow exactly the same rules as \(s, v, a\).
  • A point on a spinning body: \(v=\omega r\) and \(a_t=\alpha r\), both ⟂ to the radius, plus \(a_n=\omega^2r\) towards the axis.
  • Gears and belts share \(v\) and \(a_t\) at the contact, not \(a_n\).
